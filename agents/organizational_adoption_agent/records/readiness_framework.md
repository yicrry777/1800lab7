# Organizational Adoption Readiness Framework

## Decision supported

The framework determines whether an organization is ready to pilot, publish, and sustain multimodal generative-AI short-video content. It does not decide whether the technology is strategically attractive in the abstract.

## Current-state profile

| Dimension | Evidence sought | Readiness question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leadership and sponsorship | Named executive, scope, risk tolerance, escalation path | Can leaders make and enforce bounded adoption decisions? |
| Skills and training | Operator/reviewer skills, legal and risk access, evaluation training | Can people use and challenge outputs competently? |
| Workflow and process | Approved inputs, human approval, segregation, records, rollback | Can the organization prevent and correct unsafe publication? |
| Infrastructure and data | Approved vendor, access control, logs, asset management, provenance retention | Can the technical workflow preserve security, traceability, and evidence? |
| Resources and budget | Capacity for review, controls, training, measurement, operations | Can the organization sustain the application beyond buying a license? |
| Governance and risk | Use boundaries, rights, privacy/security, disclosure, monitoring, incident response | Are material risks assigned and controlled? |
| Culture and change | Incentives, workload, role acceptance, challenge culture | Will the operating model be adopted rather than bypassed? |
| Operating ownership | Service, control, incident, metrics, vendor-change, and funding owners | Who keeps the system safe and useful after launch? |

Ratings are 3 (fit for controlled pilot), 2 (partial), 1 (absent/critical), and 0 (unknown). Ratings are not averaged; a critical gate can constrain the overall decision.

## Overall classifications

- **Ready for bounded pilot:** all application-specific gates are evidenced; exit criteria and owners are set.
- **Conditionally ready:** a limited pilot is supportable, but stated gates must close before publication or scale.
- **Not ready:** the proposed timing exposes critical control, ownership, or capacity gaps.
- **Indeterminate:** the case lacks evidence needed for a defensible decision.

## Action quality test

Every recommended action must answer five questions: Which observed gap does it close? What is its priority? Who owns it? By when or before which decision? What evidence proves completion?
